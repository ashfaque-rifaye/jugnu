# Demo film: what to record

You record three things: your **officer lines**, your **narration**, and the **phone call**. I do the rest.

## 1. Your voice recordings

Save each line as its own file, named as shown (V1, V2, N1 and so on), in this folder:

`D:\Projects\Hackathon - AI for Foundational Learning\Jugnu (Firefly)\demo\voice\`

**How to record:**
- Use your phone's voice recorder, in a quiet room with fans off. Any format works: m4a, mp3 or wav.
- Hold the phone at a normal distance and speak at a calm, normal pace.
- Leave about a second of silence before and after each line.
- If you trip over a word, just record that line again. The newest file is the only one I need.

## 2. The phone call

**You play the teacher, and Jugnu rings your phone for real.** Tell me when you're ready and I'll start the take. Your phone will ring a few seconds after your V3 line plays. The call comes from **080 4680 0010**.

**Before I start:**
- Turn on Do Not Disturb, so no notifications appear in your recording. Check that it still lets this call through.
- Start your phone's screen recording before the phone rings, and keep it running until the call ends.
- Sit somewhere quiet. Use the earpiece or wired earphones, not the loudspeaker.

**About the sound:** many phones don't record call audio in a screen recording, and that's fine. Jugnu's side keeps its own copy of the call, so I'll use that for the sound and your recording for the picture.

**During the call:**
- Wait for Jugnu to finish before you speak, then leave a second of silence after you finish. That silence tells Jugnu you're done.
- The first question comes in English and then in Hindi. **Wait for the Hindi part to end, then say "English".**
- It will call you by the school's teacher name, which is a made-up demo name. That's expected.

**What to say.** Jugnu's questions change with your answers, so go by the topic rather than the order:

| When Jugnu says or asks about | You say |
|---|---|
| Hindi or English? | English. |
| Shall we begin? | Yes, please go ahead. |
| The first half hour of school | Every morning, for the first forty minutes, I group children by reading level, not by class. Letter, word, paragraph and story groups. |
| Materials, or what the groups do | Each group has its own cards. Children read to a partner, and I sit with the group that needs me most. |
| Children at different levels | Every Monday I listen to each child read for two minutes, and I move them to a new group if they are ready. |
| Maths, counting, adding | We use bundles of sticks to teach tens, and do fifteen minutes of oral sums every day. |
| Children who don't speak Hindi at home | Many children speak Bundeli at home, so I start in Bundeli and then move to Hindi. |
| Children who miss school | Their partner shows them the notebook the next day and helps them catch up. |
| Parents | Every Saturday, children read one page aloud to a parent, and I send parents a one minute voice note. |
| The one most important thing | The first forty minutes in level groups. That is what matters most. |
| Anything else | Give a short, honest-sounding answer with a number in it, such as how many minutes or how often. |

The whole call takes about 5 to 6 minutes. **Stay on until Jugnu thanks you and the call ends**, because that's when it turns the call into methods. In the film I'll cut it down to the best moments, and the narration says you're playing the teacher.

If a take goes wrong, just hang up and tell me. We can redo it.
